Everything you wanted to know about mountaineering, climbing gear, safety and expeditions.

Mountaineering is the activity of climbing mountains, usually to reach the summit. It combines hiking, rock climbing and climbing on snow and ice, and it often takes several days in remote places where weather and altitude make it challenging.

Beginners need sturdy waterproof boots, warm layered clothing, a waterproof jacket, a backpack, a helmet, gloves, sunglasses and sunscreen, a map and compass or GPS, a headlamp, a first-aid kit, plus enough food and water. On snow and ice you also need crampons, an ice axe, a harness and rope.

The main types are trekking or hillwalking, rock climbing, ice climbing, alpine climbing (mixed rock, snow and ice on high peaks) and high-altitude expedition climbing on the world's biggest mountains. Climbs can also be done in "expedition style" with fixed camps, or the faster, lighter "alpine style".

Climbers need good fitness and stamina, navigation with a map and compass, rope work and knots, belaying, walking with crampons, using an ice axe to stop a fall, basic first aid, and the ability to read the weather and make safe decisions as a team.

The biggest dangers are avalanches, falling rocks and ice, hidden crevasses, sudden storms, extreme cold and frostbite, and altitude sickness. Tiredness and poor decisions near the summit also cause many accidents.

Preparation starts months ahead with cardio and strength training and practice climbs on smaller peaks. On the mountain, climbers acclimatise slowly by going higher during the day and sleeping lower ("climb high, sleep low"), drink plenty of water and learn the warning signs of altitude sickness.

Trekking means walking long distances on trails through mountain areas, usually without special climbing gear. Mountaineering means actually climbing up a mountain, often on steep rock, snow or ice, using ropes, crampons and ice axes and needing more technical skill.

Look for an alpine or mountaineering club in your city, country or university, or ask at a local climbing wall or outdoor shop. Most clubs welcome beginners, organise group trips and run training courses. You usually fill in a membership form and pay a small yearly fee.

The five highest are Mount Everest (8,849 m), K2 (8,611 m), Kangchenjunga (8,586 m), Lhotse (8,516 m) and Makalu (8,485 m). All fourteen mountains above 8,000 metres are in the Himalaya and Karakoram ranges of Asia.

Always check the weather, tell someone your route and return time, and never climb alone. Carry the right gear and a first-aid kit, wear a helmet, stay roped up on glaciers, go up slowly to avoid altitude sickness, and be ready to turn back if conditions become unsafe.
